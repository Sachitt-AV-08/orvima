What most browser agents do

  • Report ok: true the moment the click is dispatched
  • Fail silently when the button was disabled, hidden or covered
  • Let a purchase or a deletion through unattended
  • Send your session to a vendor to get results

What orvima does

  • Re-reads the DOM afterwards and reports verified
  • Returns verified: false when it could not confirm
  • Holds irreversible actions behind a fail-closed gate
  • Runs entirely on 127.0.0.1, against your own browser

The gate is the point.

Any MCP-capable AI can be told to click. The difference is what happens when the thing in front of it is a delete button. orvima's gate stops, shows a human what is about to happen, and refuses to proceed unless the page still looks the same when they answer.

  1. 1

    The agent asks.

    A tool call is classified. Routine work — navigating, reading, filling a form — is not interrupted.

  2. 2

    It stops, and says why.

    The pending action carries its risk, the page title, the URL and the element's own text. A decision you cannot check is not a decision.

  3. 3

    You answer.

    From the local UI, or from your phone over your own network. No token, no buttons are rendered at all.

  4. 4

    The page is re-checked.

    Your approval referred to the page as it looked when it was queued. orvima classifies once more against the page as it is now, so an approval cannot be spent on something that has since been replaced.

  5. 5

    Decisions expire.

    Come back in an hour and the card is gone, with the reason. A stale approval is not something you can act on.

Real capture

The gate sees the selector, judges it outward, queues for human, and the click never runs.

Fails closed, including on a broken install.

If the gate cannot be built, orvima does not disable it — it gates everything, and says why. A malfunction must never have the same effect as a decision to turn safety off.

Questions before you install

The questions that decide whether an agent tool survives past a demo.

Does it need an API key?

No. Install it and connect it to Claude Desktop, Cursor or Copilot. Autonomous runs can use any OpenAI-compatible brain, including local Ollama.

Is my data sent to a vendor?

No. Everything runs on 127.0.0.1 against your own browser. Cookies, sessions and scrapes never leave your machine.

Can it act without me?

Routine work runs freely. Outward actions (delete, purchase, send) are held by a fail-closed gate and expire if unanswered.

Is it free?

Yes. MIT licensed, self-hosted, no account. You own every byte and every commit.
